The BRIDGE survey

Baryonic Enrichment in Diffuse Gaseous Environments

The BRIDGE survey traces the baryon and metal cycles between galaxies and the gas that surrounds them, connecting the circumgalactic medium (CGM) and the intergalactic medium (IGM) at Cosmic Noon ($z \sim 1$–$2.2$), the epoch when star formation and black hole growth in the Universe peaked.

Motivation

Galaxies are not closed systems. Gas flows in along filaments, is processed into stars, and is returned—enriched with metals and energy—to the surrounding medium through winds and outflows. The diffuse gas that mediates this exchange, spanning the CGM out to the IGM, holds the majority of a galaxy's baryonic and metal budget. Comprehensive surveys on the enrichment of the cool, multi-phase CGM exist at low ($z \lesssim 1$; CCC) and high ($2.2\leq z \leq 3.6$; KODIAQ-Z) redshifts. Yet, at the most active epoch ($1 \leq z \leq 2.2$), the metal distribution of the CGM remains the least is studied.

The BRIDGE survey uses absorption-line spectroscopy of background quasars to map this gas in column density, kinematics, and metallicity as a function of redshift and overdensity.

The metal budget

A central goal of the survey is to close the baryonic and metal budget in the CGM and IGM at Cosmic Noon $1 \leq z \leq 2.2$. Our first paper in this series summarizes existing metal mass density estimates for the various metal reservoirs of the Universe at $z < 1$ and $z > 2.2$. With metallicities estimates for hundreds of CGM and IGM absorbers with the BRIDGE survey, we will for the first time study the evolving global metal budget of the Universe for period spannning nearly 12 Gyrs or over 90% of the age of the Universe.
